Changelog #

0.41.0 - 2026-11-08 #

A minor release on 0.40.0 for numeric ranges in every selector (router[10-12]), targets that create the names a range spells, runs limited to the inventory's devices (targets.inventory-only, and --inventory, --inv, or --i, with files given on the line), the removal of security.require-fips and of karvi version's fips_mode, a karvi-sysutil that never acts as root on what the service account owns, the release's number written once in VERSION, and the shell-script review's fixes to the suites and scripts. The configuration registry moves to 34 and the audit to 4; every other counter is 0.40.0's (configuration schema 6, execution plan 17, command record 5, plan report 4, scoreboard 5, daemon IPC 12, job manifest and summary 5, credential package 2, metrics 1, inventory 1). Go and every module are 0.40.0's. A site has something to change where a configuration file, --set, or a KARVI__ variable names security.require-fips; a tool reads the audit policy's fips_required, reads the audit trail as schema 3, or reads fips_mode from karvi version or a job manifest's app.build; a selector, a credential CSV row, or a map rule holds a bracket of digits meant as a one-character class ([123], [05], [1,3]) or one now refused ([1-], [10, 12]); or a target holding a range was meant to select from the inventory alone, as it now does under --i or targets.inventory-only. An operator's 0.40.0 daemon, of another build, is drained by the first submission after the upgrade.

  • The release's number is written once, in VERSION. A package at the module root embeds the file, and karvi version reports it through buildinfo.Version, so go build, go test, and make build give the same number. The Makefile's VERSION, which nothing read, and each verifier's typed copy of the number are gone: scripts/verify-shipped.sh checks the shipped executable's version and the manifest's against the file, as scripts/verify-release.sh checks its build's. A release changes VERSION, the CHANGELOG, and CHECKSUMS.sha256. docs/DESIGN.md, section 15, has the rule.
  • The daemon-upgrade suite builds its fixture before it starts it. scripts/daemon-upgrade-smoke-test.sh ran the lifecycle fixture through go run and gave it two seconds to bind its socket, so on a cold build cache the compile took them and the suite stopped with schema-2 fixture did not start, the rest of its lane unrun. It builds tools/daemon-fixture once into its work directory and starts the executable, so the wait measures the start alone, and the pid it holds is the fixture's own.
  • The spool suite asks no floor of the host and says when S9's narrowing row cannot run. scripts/spool-smoke-test.sh ran under the default 2 GiB output.min-free-bytes-after-job, so a volume short of that and the suite's own writes refused a row with output_preflight_space (S6, with 2.8 GB free when the suite began), and S9's narrowing row, a job at a 1 GiB command limit, asked a further GiB (S9, with 3.4 GB free when the suite began, asking 3,228,434,432 bytes), stopping its lane before the job list and audit suites. The suite sets the floor to 0, as the other suites do, S10 still setting its own; S9 reads the floor from karvi and the volume's free space at the row, and runs the row when it holds the floor and two limits (one for the job at width one, one for its other files and the other lanes' writes), else prints a line naming both figures.
  • Four more suites ask no floor of the host. The audit, job list, parity, and transcript suites ran their jobs and recorded logins under the default 2 GiB output.min-free-bytes-after-job, so on a volume short of it each stopped at its first job with output_preflight_space, the transcript suite with no line. Each sets the floor to 0, as the other suites do, through the places file every suite now passes first (the transcript suite's floor rows still set their own).
  • Breaking: security.require-fips is removed; registry 34, audit schema 4. Its documentation said "Startup verifies build/runtime mode", and nothing read it but the audit, which copied it into each event's policy as fips_required: a site that set it to true ran exactly as before, its audit saying FIPS was required. A configuration file or --set naming the key is refused, config_unknown_key, and KARVI__SECURITY__REQUIRE_FIPS, config_unknown_environment; the audit's policy no longer carries fips_required. karvi has no FIPS 140-3 mode; what one waits on is in ROADMAP.md.
  • Breaking: karvi version has no fips_mode line. The value was the Makefile's FIPS_MODE word, stamped in at build time and checked against nothing: false for a build with Go's FIPS 140-3 mode on (GOFIPS140, or GODEBUG=fips140=on), true for an ordinary build made with FIPS_MODE=true. The line, the version JSON's fips_mode, a job manifest's app.build.fips_mode, and the Makefile's FIPS_MODE are gone; the job schema stays 5, the manifest's app being free-form.
  • karvi-sysutil never acts as root on what the service account owns. The account owns its home and could replace the home, .config, or config.toml with a link. daemon install's repair then gave the link's target to the account as root: a config.toml linked to /etc/shadow made it the account's, mode 0600, and a .config linked to /etc set /etc to mode 0700, closing it to every other user, and gave the account the global layer. daemon remove removed config.toml through such a link, taking /etc/karvi/config.toml, the site's global file, with it. Root now makes only the home itself, and the configuration and its folders are made, repaired, and removed as the account. Before anything changes, both refuse a home the install did not make, and a link, another type, or another owner's in place of the home, .config, .config/karvi, or the configuration, what was found shown over what is wanted. The account joins the operators' group before its home is made, since it reaches the home through that group's users directory, and the record lists membership before home. A removal the account cannot carry out (removed by hand, or out of the operators' group) is refused; daemon install takes the account back first. scripts/sysutil-smoke-test.sh plants each link and checks that the link's target and the host stay as they were.
  • karvi-sysutil stops when it cannot write its record. A refusal to write /etc/karvi/service-karvi.installed ended only a pipeline, so a full or read-only /etc/karvi let the install go on and exit 0, the record short of what it made; the refusal now ends the run, exit 1, and removes its temporary file. A removal that empties the record, after an install refused at the account step with /etc/karvi already there, no longer reports that it cannot write it or leaves a .service-karvi.* file behind.
  • karvi-sysutil reads daemon status without colour. Under a global file's display.color = "always", the install read a painted compatible: value and warned of "another build" on every run.
  • The scripts end at a signal, their cleanup run once. Twenty-four scripts set their cleanup with trap ... EXIT HUP INT TERM, so a Ctrl-C, TERM, or HUP ran the cleanup and the script went on, running it again at its end: a Ctrl-C during scripts/device-qualification.sh against the fake stopped the fake, and the run went on through 77 more rows, 46 of them failing, to exit 1. make generated-clean's trap was on EXIT alone, which dash (/bin/sh) does not run when a signal ends the shell, so a Ctrl-C left its scratch folder in /tmp. Every script and the recipe set their cleanup through on_exit in scripts/lib/exit.sh: the cleanup runs once, and HUP, INT, and TERM end the script with 129, 130, and 143. The five bash scripts whose trap was on EXIT alone use it too, so a TERM sent to one of them alone no longer runs its cleanup while the command it waits on is still running. scripts/lib/exit-test.sh, which run_suites runs first with the JSON reader's test, ends a model script under sh and bash with each signal, and fails when a script sets a trap on EXIT any other way. ROADMAP's item for it is closed.
  • A Ctrl-C stops every lane of the suites. run_suites started its three lanes as asynchronous commands, which a script starts with INT ignored, so a Ctrl-C ended the verifier and left the lanes running: 45 s into a run, the verifier ended at once and, 15 s later, the v0100 and smoke suites and their daemons were still running, the lane folder left in TMPDIR. Each lane runs in a session of its own (setsid); HUP, INT, and TERM while the lanes run send TERM to each lane's group, so each suite runs its cleanup, wait up to 30 s for the groups to empty, a second signal meanwhile ignored (a second TERM would cut the suites' cleanups short), then print the lanes' output, remove the lane folder, and end the verifier with 129, 130, or 143. The same Ctrl-C now ends the run in under a second with nothing left. Once the lanes have ended, the signal traps are on_exit's again, through signal_exits in scripts/lib/exit.sh, so run_suites refuses a caller that has not sourced exit.sh before anything runs, and scripts/verify-release.sh sources it before the suites. scripts/lib/suites-test.sh, which run_suites runs first with the other library tests, runs a model verifier under sh and bash over model suites in a scratch root. The library tests start their models as a terminal starts a job, through terminal_job (setsid env --default-signal=INT), so scripts/lib/exit-test.sh no longer needs python3; BUILD-HOWTO §1 names setsid(1) among util-linux's tools.
  • A suite's cleanup stops its daemon with --force and always reaches its removal. The parity and spool suites stopped the daemon in their cleanup through a function whose fail is exit 1, so a daemon still listening after its stop ended the cleanup there: against a model daemon that ignores the stop, a suite whose rows had passed ended 1 after 10 s, its work folder and its fake left. The smoke, v080, v090, and canary suites and the lifecycle replay stopped it without --force, which a daemon holding a job refuses (daemon_active_jobs, exit 112), so after a Ctrl-C in the middle of a row the folder went and the daemon ran on: against the released build, it finished its job and wrote the removed work folder again (its scoreboard, capacity lock, and the job's summary). Every suite and lab that starts a daemon or a fake stops it through scripts/lib/lab.sh: daemon_stop_force SOCKET COMMAND... runs daemon stop --force, waits up to 15 s for the socket to go, and when it stays names it on stderr and returns 1, never exiting; fake_stop VAR stops the fake and empties its pid. A cleanup goes on to its removal (the same model: the suite's own 0, the line, the folder removed, the fake stopped; the same job: stopped in 0.6 s, nothing left), and a row that needs the daemon gone fails on the status, among them smoke's stop before its idle-timer daemon, whose status was ignored. The device qualification's stop after each daemon row waits for the socket before the row's folder moves into the evidence, and records a daemon that stays as a failed check. scripts/lib/lab-test.sh, which run_suites runs with the other library tests, runs both functions under sh and bash against a model daemon, and fails when a script ignores a daemon stop's status or stops a daemon in its cleanup other than through daemon_stop_force.
  • The checks that karvi writes nothing under the home watch the passwd entry's home. scripts/verify-shipped.sh's stateless --help and version and the completion suite's hidden words looked for writes under a fresh $HOME, but karvi takes its home from the passwd entry, so a write into the operator's own ~/.local/share/karvi passed them: a model executable that made its private root under a folder standing for the passwd home passed verify-shipped's lines as they were. Both compare karvi's places under the passwd entry's home (~/.local/share/karvi, ~/.config/karvi, by their existence) and the host's shared places before and after, through host_places and host_places_unchanged in scripts/lib/host.sh; verify-shipped makes its work folder, its cleanup set, before the stateless runs, so a failed check or a signal leaves none of its folders behind, and ROADMAP's item for it is closed. scripts/lib/host-test.sh, which run_suites runs with the other library tests, runs both functions under sh and bash over a scratch home against a model executable, never the operator's home.
  • A guard of several tests fails on any of them. set -e ignores a failure before the last command of an && list, so two guards checked only their last test: scripts/verify-shipped.sh passed whole with bin/karvi-askpass-linux-amd64 at mode 0644, and the daemon-upgrade suite, given a version without its daemon_ipc_schema line, went past its guard and stopped rows later with no message. Each of verify-shipped's four tests is its own line; the suite's guard names the version, IPC schema, and build when one is missing.
  • Every must-not-appear check stops its suite. The v061 suite's check was a bare ! grep, which set -e ignores, so a help naming --address as the alias for --target passed it. It goes through absent PATTERN FILE, now in scripts/lib/lab.sh, whose five copies in the transcript, display, v070, v080, and v090 suites are gone; absent also stops at a file it cannot read, which the copies passed. scripts/lib/lab-test.sh runs it under sh and bash and fails on a copy of it or a bare negated pipeline in a script.
  • The parity suite's last leak check sees a daemon left running. It looked for daemon serve.*$TMP, but a daemon's command line holds its --config and --set before daemon serve, so a model daemon left running passed it. It looks for --config $TMP/karvi.toml .*daemon serve.
  • The transcript suite's first refusal row runs under the suite's places. The --debug-show-secrets row ran karvi without the suite's places and fake transport, so a regression of the refusal would have recorded under the operator's basedir and reached the host's port 22. It runs through the suite's $COMMON, as the rows after it.
  • The spool suite runs no karvi-prune. Its S5 ran karvi-prune --days 0, refused at its arguments since 0.39.0 (exit 2), its status unread, so the row held because nothing ran. The row is removed, not repaired: karvi-prune walks the jobs, transcripts, and scoreboard folders, never the spool, and the Go tests cover what it leaves inside them. S5 keeps the admission sweep, and the suite no longer needs karvi-prune beside the executable.
  • scripts/lib/json.sh's examples count the outcome karvi writes. Its two examples counted run.end with completed; the word is complete.
  • karvi-crun's lock is under the operator's private root. It was ${TMPDIR:-/tmp}/karvi-crun.<uid>.lock, a name another user can make first: on a shared jump box, a file alice made at bob's name refused every tick of bob's (cannot create ... Permission denied, exit 2, under the host's protected_regular), and on a host without it a file she held locked would skip them all; a hand run with TMPDIR set took another lock than the cron's and ran beside the tick. The lock is <basedir>/state/crun.lock, the private root karvi resolves from the passwd home (config show --explain basedir), so the cron, a hand run, and sudo -u take one lock. A first tick makes the root's missing folders as karvi's first activity does (0750, state 0700) and never makes an explicit basedir; a basedir karvi refuses, or an explicit one that is not a directory, ends the tick with exit 1 and one line. KARVI_CRUN_LOCK still names another path. The parity suite's lone tick takes the default lock.
  • The source bundle and the package stop when tar fails. scripts/package-source-bundle.sh piped tar into gzip and scripts/build-deb.sh tar into tar, under /bin/sh without pipefail, so a file tar could not read was left out and the script went on: in a clone holding an unreadable file, the bundle script wrote a bundle without it and printed its checksum, exit 0, and make deb built a package from the short copy, exit 0. Each writes tar's archive to a file first and stops with exit 1 when tar fails; the bundle script removes its output first, so no bundle is left.
  • The source bundle's bytes no longer follow the checkout's umask. Its owner, group, and mtime were fixed and its modes were not: clones made under umask 0022, 0002, 0027, and 0077 gave four bundles (the 0.40.0 bundle carries 0664 and 0775). The bundle's tar sets every mode from the owner's execute bit alone (--mode=a+rX,u+w,go-w: files 0644, executables and folders 0755, the two modes git keeps) and names its format (gnu, tar's default), so all four give one; the next release's bundle carries 0644 and 0755, so its bytes differ from what the same tree gave before.
  • The Makefile's smoke targets are removed. smoke, halt-smoke, k03-smoke, v0100-smoke, native-smoke, and canary-smoke each depended on build, so running one overwrote bin/'s executables, a release's qualified build among them. A suite runs by its shebang against bin/ or the executable KARVI names, and run_suites runs them all; build, and all through it, alone writes bin/'s executables. tools-build (bin/secret-scan) stays and joins .PHONY, which also lacked v0100-smoke and canary-smoke. BUILD-HOWTO and BUILD-QUALIFICATION name the suite's own command.
  • karvi-prune(8) names the final statuses karvi writes. It listed completed, halted, errored, cancelled, and exercised; the words are complete, halt, error, cancel, incomplete, and exercise (records.FinalStatuses), as docs/PRUNE.md has them.
  • The spool suite's cut rows cut inside the stream. S4's cancel and lost session sent SIGINT to the client, and killed the fake device, 0.7 s after the client started. On the build host the 65.7 MB answer had not begun by then, so both records held 0 bytes and the rows passed, their status, digest, and count checks holding for an empty output; a client started a second late took the SIGINT before karvi ran and exited 0. Each row now waits for a beat of the scoreboard counting the target's bytes (up to 20 s), then cuts, and checks that the record holds bytes: 12 to 31 MB on the build host.
  • Three more suites wait on what a row needs, not a fixed time. The job list suite killed its run 2 s after the start, so a run slow to reserve its folder left the exercise's folder taken for it; it waits for the run's folder and the summary written at acceptance. Its folder without a summary was the last ID of the killed run's second, so a run made in that second sorted below it and was missed; the folder is the ID after the killed run's. The v0100 suite's i1 interrupted a one-second command a second after the client started, 0.3 s to spare; the device holds it three seconds, as for the shutdown rows. The sysutil suite checked 13 s after the operator's logout for the file logind removes 10 s after it; it waits up to 30 s and reports the time.
  • The ICMP rows skip only on a host karvi reports without a method. The canary suite's p1 to p3 and the v0100 suite's g1 to g7 read icmp_method from karvi version --format json with sed, and skipped when the read found nothing, so the version JSON laid out with a space before each colon, or the key moved into an object, skipped the rows and both suites passed on every host. They read it with json_get, skip on unavailable alone, and fail on any other answer, the key's absence included.
  • The suites read JSON by its path, not its text. Forty-one lines of eleven scripts (the canary, k03, v0100, transcript, v080, display, parity, and sysutil suites, the qualification, the output scale run, and scripts/verify-shipped.sh) matched a document's text with grep or sed, which holds for one layout and finds a key at any depth: the canary suite's audit check took any field named username for the device's user, and its report checks a key of the name anywhere in the report. Each reads through scripts/lib/json.sh, a JSON Lines stream line by line, the transcript suite's start record field by field; the sysutil suite reads the job's summary on the host, the container having no python3. A daemon's pid is read through daemon_pid in scripts/lib/lab.sh (the canary and v0100 suites, the output scale run, the replay). scripts/lib/json-test.sh fails on a grep, sed, or awk whose pattern names a JSON key, or a JSON stream piped into one, and scripts/lib/lab-test.sh on a daemon's pid read outside daemon_pid.
  • The suites refuse a host whose configuration reaches karvi. A karvi run reads the site's global file (/etc/karvi/config.toml or /opt/karvi/config.toml) and the operator's (~/.config/karvi/config.toml under the home of the passwd entry) beneath a suite's --config and --set, and an inherited KARVI__ variable above them, so on a configured host the suites tested the site's configuration: a global file's [telnet] read-timeout = "120s" failed the v090 suite's default row, its [config-lock] on sharedroot refused the suite's --set with config_lock_violation, and an inherited KARVI__ variable that names no key failed scripts/verify-shipped.sh's removed-key rows with config_unknown_environment. host_config_clean in scripts/lib/host.sh refuses such a host before a script makes anything, naming on its own line each file in the sources of karvi config show --format json and each inherited KARVI__ variable; every suite and lab, the three verifiers, and run_suites before its lanes call it. A configured host verifies the bundle on a build host or in a container with an init (docker run --init; docs/BUILD-QUALIFICATION.md), and scripts/device-qualification.sh runs from a host and account where karvi reads no configuration (docs/DEVICE-QUALIFICATION-RUNBOOK.md). scripts/lib/host-test.sh checks the function against a fake karvi and fails on a script sourcing host.sh, or a suite, that does not call it first; scripts/lib/suites-test.sh checks that run_suites refuses before any lane starts, and runs its model verifier, as host-test.sh runs its rows, under env -i.
  • The library tests pass on a host with no shared entry. On such a host (a container, where the new rule sends a configured host's verification) host_shared_entries lists nothing, and host_entries_diff wrote the empty listing as a blank line, so every difference also named a blank entry and six of scripts/lib/host-test.sh's rows failed, stopping run_suites before its first suite; the build host passed only because /tmp/karvi-<uid> was there. An empty listing is no line.
  • The suites keep their places in one file. Fourteen suites and labs listed the private and shared roots, the spool, the scoreboards, the capacity root, the audit file, and the floor as --set arguments, and eight in their own configuration files, each in its own layout; the floor alone took three mechanisms, the parity suite's riding the environment layer because its rows write their own [output] tables. lab_places in scripts/lib/lab.sh writes them into one file, everything under the suite's base, and every suite passes it first with --config, its own file and --set rows after: each --config is its own layer, so a later one outranks it key by key and a suite's own tables never meet a second header. A row whose subject is another root or another floor keeps its own setting and says so on its line. The replay passes the file to the prior release too; v0.28.0 and v0.29.0 read every key in it. The output scale run, the replay, and scripts/device-qualification.sh take the same file, so they too ask no floor of the host beyond each job's estimate; the qualification keeps it in its evidence as places.toml, beside each run's own TAG.karvi.toml, and each run's base now holds its scoreboards and spool (docs/DEVICE-QUALIFICATION-RUNBOOK.md). scripts/lib/lab-test.sh fails on a place key named outside the file.
  • The suites share their builds, their fake device's start, their newest job, and their row runner. Thirteen build lines in eight scripts, six copies of the fake device's start and port wait, fourteen lookups of the newest job folder, and three row runners with their failure counters are lab_build, fake_start, and newest_job in scripts/lib/lab.sh and row, row_open, fail, and finish in the new scripts/lib/rows.sh, each checked by the test beside it under sh and bash. Every helper is built as the Makefile builds the release, static and with -trimpath; a lookup that finds no job folder stops its suite with a line saying so, where an empty path failed later at a file it could not read; the k03 suite prints a line per row, as the canary and v0100 suites do, and ends k03-smoke-test: pass (N rows); the completion suite and the replay count their failures through fail and finish. The library's lines print their paths, patterns, and messages with printf, since dash's echo read a backslash in them as an escape (absent named a pattern \b as a backspace). scripts/lib/lab-test.sh and scripts/lib/rows-test.sh fail on a copy outside the library.
  • The bundle and release verifiers watch the operator's own places. Around the Go tests and the suites they compared the host's shared places alone, so a test or a suite that wrote the operator's private root or configuration under the passwd home (.local/share/karvi, .config/karvi) passed them. Both compare host_places of the passwd home, as scripts/verify-shipped.sh and the completion suite do, and host_shared_unchanged, which nothing else called, is removed; scripts/lib/host-test.sh fails on either verifier comparing less.
